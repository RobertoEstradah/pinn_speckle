\chapter*{Abstract}

\vspace*{-8mm}

Physics-Informed Neural Networks with sinusoidal activation functions
(SIREN-PINNs) are evaluated for solving the Helmholtz equation in one and two
dimensions as a foundation for optical speckle simulation. The proposed SIREN
architecture ($5 \times 128$, $\omega_0 = 1.0$)
achieves relative $L^2$ errors of $0.006\%$ (1D) and $0.171\%$ (2D) against
analytical solutions, several orders of magnitude below the $5\%$ thesis
threshold.
The speckle extension uses a spatially correlated random-phase screen and an
independent angular-spectrum propagation reference. The reference ensemble of
64 realizations gives a contrast of $C=0.9732$ at $z=20\lambda$; validation of
the PINN against this reference remains in progress.
A two-phase Adam~+~L-BFGS optimization strategy and Latin Hypercube Sampling
(LHS) are used in the training protocol.
Reproducibility was verified with three independent random seeds, yielding a
component-wise mean $L^2$ error of $0.192 \pm 0.089\%$ (population standard
deviation).

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Physics-Informed Neural Networks, SIREN,
Helmholtz equation, optical speckle, sinusoidal activation, Latin Hypercube
Sampling.
