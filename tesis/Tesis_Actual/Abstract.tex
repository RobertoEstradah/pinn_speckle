\chapter*{Abstract}

\vspace*{-8mm}

Physics-Informed Neural Networks with sinusoidal activation functions
(SIREN-PINNs) are applied to solve the Helmholtz equation, toward the goal of
simulating optical speckle patterns via a complex electric field and a
random-phase boundary condition. The proposed SIREN architecture
($5 \times 128$, $\omega_0 = 1.0$) achieves relative $L^2$ errors of
$0.006\%$ (1D) and $0.171\%$ (2D) against analytical solutions, several
orders of magnitude below the $5\%$ thesis threshold.
A two-phase Adam~+~L-BFGS optimization strategy and Latin Hypercube Sampling
(LHS) are key to convergence.
Reproducibility was verified with three independent random seeds for the 2D
case, yielding a mean $L^2$ error of $0.192 \pm 0.089\%$.
The speckle-generation stage (random-phase boundary, statistical validation
against Goodman's criteria) is in progress and not yet part of the validated
results reported here.

\bigskip

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Physics-Informed Neural Networks, SIREN,
Helmholtz equation, optical speckle, sinusoidal activation, Latin Hypercube
Sampling.
